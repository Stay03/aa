# Chelsea in finals: English vs non-English referees

**Source:** the `Club_Appearances` and `Contested_Decisions` tabs of `finals_dataset.xlsx`.
**Scope:** Chelsea played 47 finals, 1997 to 2026.

## Headline numbers

| | English referee | Non-English referee |
|---|---|---|
| Finals | 32 | 15 |
| Won | 14 (44%, 95% CI 28–61%) | 10 (67%, 95% CI 42–85%) |
| Won excluding shootouts | 13/25 (52%) | 8/10 (80%) |
| Shootouts won | 1/7 | 2/5 |
| Opponent another of the five big clubs | 24 of 32 (won 8) | 4 of 15 (won 2) |
| Opponent foreign | 0 | 11 (won 8) |
| Contested decisions for / against Chelsea | 16 / 23 | 3 / 3 |

- **Is the win-rate gap significant?** No. Fisher exact p = 0.21 for all finals and 0.25 excluding shootouts. After correction for multiple comparisons, p = 1.0.
- **Is the 16/23 split of contested decisions under English referees significant?** No: sign test p = 0.34.

## Why the comparison is mostly not about referees

1. **Different competitions.**
   - Every English-referee final was domestic: 13 FA Cup, 8 League Cup, 11 Shields.
   - Every non-English-referee final was European or world. Eight of the 15 were against foreign clubs that were, on paper, often weaker.
2. **Different opponents.**
   - Under English referees Chelsea met Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester City or Manchester United 24 times.
   - Against those clubs they won 8 of 24 (33%). Against everyone else they won 6 of 8.
3. **Shields and shootouts drag down the English-referee figure.**
   - Chelsea won 3 of 11 Community Shields, where draws go straight to penalties.
   - In FA Cup and League Cup finals they won 11 of 21 (52%).
   - In shootouts under English referees they won 1 of 7.
4. **Accounting for team strength.** I used the own-Elo estimate from English league results, which is available for all 32 English-referee finals. On that basis Chelsea won 14 against 16.1 expected (exact p = 0.45). That is within normal variation.

## The 2017–2026 run

**Result.** From 2017 onward Chelsea won **1 of 11** domestic finals under English referees. Own Elo expected 4.3 wins (exact p = 0.047). The one win was the 2018 FA Cup.

**Treat it with caution:**
- **The cut-off was chosen after looking at the data.** That makes the p-value too optimistic, and it would not survive correction for multiple comparisons.
- **Four of the ten defeats were shootouts:** the 2017 Shield, 2019 League Cup, 2022 League Cup and 2022 FA Cup. Refereeing has little bearing on a shootout.
- **Chelsea were usually underdogs.** The opponents were mostly Liverpool, Manchester City and Arsenal, and the average own-Elo win chance was 39%, against 56% in 1997–2016.
- **No contested decision against Chelsea in these finals was judged wrong by an expert.** The ones against Chelsea that were disputed:
  - 2017 FA Cup: Sánchez's goal allowed.
  - 2017 Shield: Willian's penalty claim and Pedro's red card.
  - 2020 FA Cup: Kovačić's second yellow.
  - 2021 FA Cup: handball claim before Tielemans' goal.
  - 2026 FA Cup: Hato penalty claim.

  All are rated "genuinely debatable", and several have only one source. The others were judged correct by experts or BBC pundits: the 2018 FA Cup Phil Jones card and Young handball, and the 2026 Pedro/Khusanov claim. The most consequential decisions in those finals went *in Chelsea's favour*: the goals VAR disallowed for Liverpool in 2022 and 2024, both judged correct by Dermot Gallagher.

**By referee.** No English referee has more than 4 Chelsea finals. Anthony Taylor's 1 win in 4 is the lowest; Howard Webb's 3 from 3 is the highest. These numbers are far too small to mean anything.

## Conclusion

Chelsea's better record under non-English referees (67% against 44%) is not statistically significant. It is explained by what those finals were:
- European and world finals, often against weaker foreign sides;
- set against domestic finals dominated by the other big English clubs, Community Shields and shootouts.

Once opponent strength is accounted for, the English-referee record is about what was expected. The documented refereeing disputes show no consistent tilt against Chelsea that experts judged to be wrong.
